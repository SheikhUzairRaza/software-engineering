# Scrum Introduction & XP vs Scrum Comparison

---

## 🔹 Scrum Introduction

Scrum is an Agile framework for managing software development using short iterative cycles called **Sprints**.

📌 Origin:
The term “Scrum” comes from rugby, where teamwork is tightly coordinated.

---

## 🔹 Scrum Workflow

### 🟦 1. Product Backlog

- Complete list of all requirements
- Managed and prioritized by the Product Owner

---

### 🟨 2. Sprint Planning

- Selection of backlog items for next sprint
- Defines what will be built in the cycle

📌 Sprint duration:

- 1 to 30 days

---

### 🟧 3. Task Breakdown & Estimation

- Sprint items are divided into smaller tasks
- Time/effort is estimated for each task

---

### 🧑‍💻 4. Task Assignment

- Tasks are assigned to team members
- Each member is responsible for delivery

---

### 🔁 5. Daily Scrum

- 10–15 minute daily meeting

Each member answers:

- What was done yesterday?
- What will be done today?
- Any blockers?

---

### 🎯 6. Sprint Execution

- Development happens during sprint
- Working software is produced at the end

---

## 🔹 Sprint Review

- End-of-sprint demonstration
- Product is shown to stakeholders
- Feedback is collected

---

## 🔹 Sprint Retrospective

- Team reviews process performance

Focus:

- What went well
- What went wrong
- What to improve

---

## 🔹 Scrum Roles

### 👤 Product Owner

- Defines and prioritizes requirements

### 👤 Scrum Master

- Ensures process is followed
- Removes obstacles

### 👥 Development Team

- Builds the product

---

## 🔹 Scrum Artifacts

### 📌 Product Backlog

- All requirements list

### 📌 Sprint Backlog

- Selected tasks for current sprint

### 📌 Burn-down Chart

- Remaining work tracking

### 📌 Product Increment

- Working software after sprint

---

## 🔹 Scrum Principles

- Work divided into sprints
- Each sprint delivers working software
- Continuous feedback from customer
- Product always in usable state

---

## 🔹 When Scrum is NOT suitable

- Customer is not available
- Work cannot be split into small parts
- Heavy documentation is required
- System is stable and does not need iteration

---

# 🔹 XP vs Scrum Comparison

---

## 🔸 1. Focus

| XP                    | Scrum              |
| --------------------- | ------------------ |
| Engineering practices | Project management |

---

## 🔸 2. Iteration Style

- XP → 1–2 week cycles
- Scrum → 1–30 day sprints

---

## 🔸 3. Requirements Handling

- XP → User Stories
- Scrum → Product Backlog

---

## 🔸 4. Development Style

- XP → Pair programming + strict coding practices
- Scrum → Team-based structured workflow

---

## 🔸 5. Meetings

- XP → Informal communication
- Scrum → Formal meetings (Daily Scrum, Review, Retrospective)

---

## 🔸 6. Customer Involvement

- Continuous in both XP and Scrum
- Customer gives feedback and sets priorities

---

# 🔹 Similarities

- Incremental development
- Customer involvement
- Continuous feedback
- Flexibility to change
- Regular delivery of working software

# Agile, XP, and Scrum 

---

## 1. Extreme Programming (XP) – Quick Recap

XP focuses on:

* Strong customer involvement
* Continuous testing
* Continuous improvement (refactoring)
* Simple design

### Core Idea:

Build software  **step-by-step** , and improve it continuously using feedback.

---

## 2. Test-First Development (Key XP Practice)

### Concept:

* Tests are written **before code**
* Code is written only to **pass the test**

### Benefits:

* Early bug detection
* Better reliability
* Higher code quality

---

## 3. Continuous Testing

* Testing is performed  **throughout development** , not just at the end
* Every feature is verified immediately

### Result:

* Bugs are detected early
* System stability improves

---

## 4. Continuous Improvement (Refactoring)

Refactoring means:

* Improving code structure
* Making code cleaner and more efficient
* Without changing functionality

### Purpose:

* Maintain clean and maintainable code
* Improve long-term system quality

---

## 5. XP Limitation (Complacency Risk)

### Problem:

* Developers may rely too much on testing
* Ignore proper system design

### Risk:

* Weak long-term architecture

### Solution:

* Maintain balance between **testing and design**

---

## 6. When XP / Scrum May Not Be Suitable

Avoid XP or Scrum when:

* Customer is not available
* System cannot be divided into small increments
* Heavy documentation or strict design is required

---

## 7. Agile Principles (Core Foundation)

All Agile models (including XP and Scrum) follow:

1. Customer involvement
2. Incremental development
3. People over process
4. Embrace change
5. Simplicity

---

## 8. Embrace Change & Simplicity

### Embrace Change:

* Requirements can change anytime
* System adapts easily

### Simplicity:

* Avoid over-design
* Build only what is needed

---

## 9. Scrum – Customer Role

In Scrum, the customer (or product owner) is responsible for:

### Responsibilities:

* Defining product requirements
* Prioritizing features
* Participating in planning
* Reviewing completed work

### Important:

* If the customer is not involved → Scrum may fail

---

## 10. Incremental Development in Scrum

Scrum uses  **Sprints** :

* Short development cycles (1–30 days)
* Each sprint produces a **working increment**

### Key Idea:

Each sprint must add **usable value** to the product.

---

## 11. Scrum Workflow Essentials

### Key Elements:

* **Product Backlog** → List of all requirements
* **Sprint Backlog** → Tasks for current sprint
* **Sprint Review** → Demonstration to customer

---

## 12. People Over Process (Scrum Application)

Scrum prioritizes people over strict processes:

### How:

* Team decides deadlines
* Work is divided into tasks
* Responsibilities are clearly assigned
* Daily communication is encouraged

---

### Daily Scrum Meeting

Each team member answers:

* What was done yesterday?
* What will be done today?
* Any blockers?

### Purpose:

* Improve coordination
* Solve issues quickly

---

## 13. Handling Change in Scrum

* Changes are expected
* New requirements are added to future sprints

### Result:

* System evolves continuously
* Flexibility is maintained

---

## 14. Maintaining Simplicity in Scrum

Scrum minimizes unnecessary work:

### Only essential artifacts:

* Product Backlog
* Sprint Backlog
* Burndown Chart

### Focus:

* Deliver working software
* Avoid excessive documentation

---

## 15. Scrum vs XP (Conceptual Comparison)

| Aspect          | Scrum           | XP                            |
| --------------- | --------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Increment Name  | Sprint          | Increment                     |
| Requirements    | Product Backlog | User Stories                  |
| Cycle Duration  | Up to 30 days   | Shorter cycles (Upto 15 days) |
| Meetings        | Daily Scrum     | Less formal structure         |
| Coding Practice | Not defined     | Pair Programming              |

---

## 16. Common Ground (Scrum + XP)

Both models:

* Follow Agile principles
* Use incremental development
* Require customer involvement
* Deliver working software frequently

---

## 17. Agile Challenges

Common issues in Agile models:

* Customer unavailability
* Poor architectural decisions (due to minimal upfront design)

---

## 18. When to Use Agile (XP / Scrum)

Best suited for:

* Research and development projects
* Projects with changing requirements
* Small to medium-sized systems
* Freelance or client-driven work

---

## 19. Final Conclusion

* Scrum and XP are both **Agile approaches**
* They follow the same principles but use **different practices**
* Selection depends on:
  * Project size
  * Team structure
  * Customer availability

---

## One-Line Summary

> Agile is the mindset, while XP and Scrum are practical ways to build flexible, customer-driven software using small, continuous improvements.
>
